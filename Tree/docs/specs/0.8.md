## 1. Shapes on the nutrient dots

**Purpose.** Water, nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are told apart only by colour today.
Green and orange are the pair that red-green colour blindness confuses most, and the care signals
and the 0.7 glow add more colour cues.

**What the player should feel.** Nothing new. The dots read at a glance for everyone.

**Input.** None.

**Output.** Each nutrient keeps its colour and gets its own glow shape: water a drop, nitrogen
a leaf, phosphorus a four-point spark, potassium a ring. The same four marks appear wherever a
nutrient is shown by colour: dots underground, the HUD pills, the journal's care page and the
meadow-hint page, the 0.7 glow, and the bonsai pellet tins.
**0.8.1 adjustment (Simon, 2026-09-30, 17:53 UTC):** "im Baummodus keine Symbole mehr, sondern
wieder nur farbige Punkte wie zuvor". In tree mode (the HUD pills and anything else drawn over the
tree view) the nutrients show as plain coloured dots again, as before 0.8. Extended at 18:06 UTC:
"unter der Erde auch keine Formen". So in play the dots are plain coloured everywhere: tree mode,
the HUD, the root run's dots and the wish glow. Shapes remain only where a nutrient is explained
rather than played: on the journal's care and meadow-hint pages as a small key beside the
colour, and on the bonsai pellet tins' labels. **This drops the colour-blind reason for the
shapes** (section 1's purpose). What stays for it: the four colours must also differ clearly in
brightness, not only in hue, so a greyscale screenshot still tells at least water, nitrogen and
phosphorus apart; a player who needs more can still read the journal key.

**What working looks like.** A screenshot turned to greyscale still shows which dot is which.

**Soft failure.** None; it is a look.

**Edge cases.** Far dots in the fog are only a few pixels: the shape must still be readable at
the smallest size on the phone, or fall back to a round glow beyond that distance for every kind
alike. Collected (dimmed) dots keep their shape.

**Open question and choice.**
- Always on, or a pinboard switch? **Taken: always on** (recommended). No setting to find,
  and the shapes help everyone. A switch is a later option if Simon dislikes the look.

**Tuning levers.** Shape size relative to the dot, the distance where shapes fade to round.

**Dependencies.** Root view dot material (a texture atlas on the existing dot quads, no new
draw calls), HUD pills, journal pages, 0.7 glow, bonsai tins.

## 2. Save backup

**Purpose.** A month of growth, the album and the bonsai live in one local save. A lost phone,
a reinstall or a broken file would lose all of it. There is no cloud save (a pillar), so the
player needs a way to keep a copy.

**What the player should feel.** Safe and in control, without being reminded of it.

**Input.** Two pinboard notes: "copy my tree" and "load a copy". An automatic copy after each
sunrise, kept in the game's own storage.

**Output.**
- "copy my tree" opens Android's own "save as" picker (plain Android, no Google services, no
  storage permission) and writes one file: the save, the album photos and the pages read, in one
  zip, named with the tree and the date. On a PC it goes into the game folder.
- "load a copy" opens the system picker, reads the file, asks "sure? tap again" like the reset
  notes (the current game is replaced), then loads it and writes a diary line.
- Automatically, the game keeps the last three sunrise saves in its own storage. If the save
  cannot be read at start, it offers the newest good one instead of starting over.

**What working looks like.** Copy, reinstall, load: tree, roots, clock, album, species,
bonsai and journal come back exactly (a round-trip test in the headless suite).

**Soft failure.** A broken or foreign file is refused with a handwritten note; the current game
is never touched. A copy from a newer game version is refused with a note ("this copy is from a
newer Tree").

**Edge cases.** The picker is cancelled: nothing happens. Low storage: the note says so. A copy
made in the middle of a night run saves as the run's start (as the normal save does). The
automatic copies never ask anything and never notify.

**Open questions and choices.**
- Include the album photos? **Taken: yes, one zip file** (recommended). Without them a loaded
  copy would show an empty album.
- Automatic local copies too? **Taken: yes, the last three sunrises** (recommended). They
  guard against a broken save without the player doing anything.
- Remind the player to back up? **Taken: never.** No reminders or nags (pillars).

**Tuning levers.** Number of automatic copies (2 to 5).

**Dependencies.** Save data, the album store, the TreePhone Android plugin (document picker),
the pinboard and its "sure? tap again" pattern from the reset notes.

## 3. Share a photo

**Purpose.** The calm alternative to leaderboards and friends' trees (Never list): the player
can show their tree to someone the way they would show a photo of a garden.

**What the player should feel.** Pride in something they grew, shared on their own terms.

**Input.** A "send" word on a Polaroid in the album, and on a finished tree's time-lapse.

**Output.** Android's share sheet opens with the Polaroid (photo, white frame and the
handwritten caption) as an image, or the month's time-lapse as a video. The player picks the app;
the game sends nothing by itself. On a PC the file is written to the game folder and the folder
opens.

**What working looks like.** The sheet opens with the right image on the Fairphone under /e/OS,
and the image looks like the Polaroid in the album, without any HUD.

**Soft failure.** No app can take it: a note says so, and the file stays in the game folder.

**Edge cases.** The first photo of a tree (no caption yet): it shares with the date. A very old
Android: the button is hidden.

**Open questions and choices.**
- What is shared? **Taken: the whole Polaroid with its caption** (recommended), not the bare
  photo. The caption is part of the keepsake.
- Also the time-lapse video? **Taken: yes** (recommended), with the same share sheet, since
  the video is already saved to the gallery in 0.6.
- Any text, link or game name added? **Taken: none.** The picture speaks for itself; no
  advertising of the game.

**Tuning levers.** None.

**Dependencies.** The album, the time-lapse video, the TreePhone plugin (a share intent with a
file provider, plain Android).

## 4. Brush pile with a hedgehog

**Purpose.** Pruning gets a natural second life, and the clearing a new, quiet visitor that
the player caused. It pairs with the 0.7 marked branches: cutting dying wood feeds the pile.

**What the player should feel.** A small, warm surprise: "my cuttings became a home."

**Input.** None new: pruning as today.

**Output.** Cut branches no longer vanish after tipping into the grass: at the next sunrise
they lie on a low pile of sticks at the clearing edge, in view from the normal camera, on the
side away from the shed. Once enough wood has gathered, a hedgehog moves in a few days later.
It is seen at dusk, snuffling out of the pile and back, with a diary line and a sketch the first
time. Later a wren may sing from the pile by day. Both count as visitors for this tree.

**What working looks like.** The pile grows visibly with the first cuts; the hedgehog arrives
within about a week of steady pruning; nothing about it changes growth or life force.

**Soft failure.** No pruning, no pile, no hedgehog; nothing is missed or lost.

**Edge cases.** A new tree starts a fresh pile, as visitors are counted per tree. The bonsai's
cuttings do not go to the pile. Rain darkens the sticks (weather mood). Late autumn: the
hedgehog comes less often (it would be hibernating), and not at all from 20 November until
spring. The pile never grows into the camera path or the shed view.

**Open questions and choices.**
- Hedgehog or wren first? **Taken: hedgehog at dusk** (recommended), the stronger moment.
  The wren follows as a smaller, later visitor.
- Where does the pile go? **Taken: the clearing edge on the side away from the shed**, the
  same side every time, so the player knows where to look.
- A pile of the player's own branches is nature, not a man-made thing, so it keeps the "nothing
  man-made by the tree" rule.

**Tuning levers.** See tuning.md ("0.8, planned"): wood needed for the hedgehog, days until it
moves in, how often it shows, the stick count drawn.

**Dependencies.** Pruning (0.5 and 0.6.3), visitors, the diary, seasons, the clearing budget on
the phone (one merged mesh for the pile, a single small animated hedgehog).

## 5. Fixes from the 0.7 balance check (reserved)

Simon (2026-09-29, 22:42 UTC): the 0.7 check looks hard at balance and feel, and whatever it finds
goes straight into 0.8. This section is kept for those fixes, and they come before the four
features above.

- **Source:** every item from the "Balance and feel" block of the 0.7 broken list
  (specs/0.7-candidates.md, groups A to D) that fails in 0.7's full check or on the phone.
- **How each lands:** one line here per finding (what happened, then what it means, kept apart),
  the fix chosen with the recommended option, and the changed numbers in tuning.md with a
  reason. A fix that is really a new mechanic gets its own short spec first.
- **Order:** growth and nutrient balance first (they shape every day), then bonsai handling,
  then UI consistency.
- **Findings (0.7 full check, 2026-09-30; numbers in tuning.md):**
  - B2, too little phosphorus: 31 to 46 % of days at the soft floor on five species. Target: at
    most a quarter of days per species, and no single nutrient behind more than 60 % of them.
  - B3, stock too full: alder 100 %, birch 58 %, oak 55 % of days hold more than two days of one
    kind. Target: at most a third of days; alder's nitrogen is exempt (its nodule quirk), its
    water is not.
  - B4, too many patches: 27 to 43 in one night's reach. Target: about 8 to 15 fresh deposits in
    reach, reached by fewer but larger patches so each still reads on the meadow and the wish
    keeps its 1.5x size.
  - 0.7 item 3, two glows: the old glow goes out when a new one shows (0.7 spec updated).
  - C1, pellets take 3 taps: target 2 at most (pick the tin, tap the soil).
  - C1 and D5, repot slip buttons small: at least 9 mm (48 dp).
  - C4, needs not visible: dry soil shows lighter and cracked, a hungry tree pales or droops, a
    burnt tip browns, roots show at the pot's drainage hole when repotting is due.
  - Growth and oak pass: tip steering now pays; oak's 40 to 41 days never steered is inside the
    per-species limit (tuning.md item 10a).
- **Landed in 0.8** (docs/notes/balance-0.8.md, numbers in tuning.md): all targets above are
  met; B2's floor days are at most 17 %. Left for 0.8.1: a tree played entirely without roots
  (boost all day, every root ended at once) does not finish by day 45 on 3 of 18 runs.

## 6. Live picture of the tree (wallpaper and screen saver)

Added by Simon on 2026-09-29, 22:46 UTC. This pulls the live wallpaper forward from "Later or maybe"
(design doc section 11). Simon chose both surfaces on a card (22:47 UTC), sharing one animation.

**Purpose.** Carry the calm of the clearing onto the phone itself: the player's own tree, as it
stands in the game today, alive on the screen.

**What the player should feel.** "That's my tree", glanced at many times a day, never demanding
anything.

**Input.** Choosing it once in Android's own wallpaper picker, or in the screen saver settings
(Settings > Display > Screen saver on /e/OS). A pinboard note "the tree on my phone" explains how,
in one line.

**Output.** One animation used by both surfaces: only the current tree, from the game's save,
seen from the south at the album camera's framing, with sky behind it and at most the grass line
and a few bushes at its foot. No HUD, no clearing edge, no text. The crown and grass move softly
in the wind, clouds drift, and the light follows the real time of day (dawn, day, golden hour,
night with the moon's phase) and the calendar season, as in the game. The tree shown updates
when the game saves (after the game is closed), never on its own.
- **Live wallpaper** (home and lock screen): Android's WallpaperService.
- **Screen saver** (while charging or docked): Android's DreamService.

**What working looks like.** It shows the same tree as the game's last save; runs at a low frame
rate (about 10 to 15 fps, pausing when the screen is off or covered by apps); drains no more than
about 1 to 2 % battery an hour as a wallpaper on the Fairphone; no Google services.

**Soft failure.** No save yet, or the save cannot be read: the last good picture, or a young
sapling in the clearing light, never an error screen. A finished tree stays until a new one is
planted.

**Edge cases.** Home screen scrolling: the view does not pan (the tree stays centred). The lock
screen crops the top: the framing keeps the whole crown below the clock area. Dark mode: no
change, the sky follows the real time. Low battery saver: the animation drops to a still frame.
The phone renderer's limits apply (Compatibility renderer, half resolution): the wallpaper draws
only the tree mesh, a sky and a grass strip, cheaper than the game's clearing.

**Open questions and choices.**
- Wallpaper, screen saver or both? **Simon: both** (card, 22:47 UTC), one shared animation;
  he switches on what he likes in Android's settings. A plugin that renders the Godot scene inside WallpaperService (the planned
  TheOathMan Godot-Android-Live-Wallpaper plugin, or the TreePhone plugin's own service) serves
  both.
- Animated or a still picture updated daily? **Taken: animated** (Simon's words: "animiert").
- Interaction? **Taken: none.** A tap opens nothing and changes nothing; it is a picture.

**Tuning levers.** Frame rate (5 to 20), wind strength, whether night is dimmer than the game.

**Dependencies.** The save file (read-only), the tree mesh builder, sky and season code, the
TreePhone Android plugin, the album camera framing.

## What "broken" looks like for 0.8

Checked in 0.8's full review, together with the lists in tuning.md and specs/0.7-candidates.md.

**Dot shapes**
1. Any two shapes can be confused at the smallest dot size on the phone, or in a greyscale
   screenshot.
2. A place that shows a nutrient by colour lacks its shape (dots, pills, care page, hint page,
   0.7 glow, pellet tins).
3. The shapes add draw calls underground or cost frames on the phone.

**Backup**
4. A copy that is loaded back does not restore the tree, roots, clock, album, species, bonsai
   and journal exactly (round-trip test).
5. A broken, foreign or newer file changes the current game in any way.
6. Loading a copy replaces the game without "sure? tap again".
7. The game asks for a storage permission, reminds the player to back up, or needs a network.

**Sharing**
8. The shared image shows any HUD, or differs from the Polaroid in the album.
9. Anything is sent without the player picking an app, or text, a link or the game's name is
   added.
10. Sharing needs Google services or fails without a note.

**Brush pile and hedgehog**
11. The pile or the hedgehog changes growth, life force or nutrients (mood only).
12. The hedgehog shows by day, more than once a day, in late autumn after 20 November, or before
    any wood was cut.
13. The pile blocks the camera, the tree or the shed view, or costs the phone more than one extra
    draw call for the pile and one for the hedgehog.
14. Cutting just to feed the pile becomes worth it: a tree pruned for the pile finishes sooner
    than one left alone (tuning.md item 13).

**Live picture**
14b. It shows anything but the current tree, sky and at most a grass line with a few bushes
    (no HUD, clearing edge or text), or a tree other than the last save's.
14c. It runs faster than about 15 fps, keeps drawing with the screen off or covered, or drains
    more than about 2 % battery an hour as a wallpaper on the Fairphone.
14d. It needs Google services, asks for a permission, or shows an error instead of a fallback
    picture when there is no readable save.
14e. The lock screen clock covers the crown, or the view pans with home screen scrolling.

**App icon**
14f. At 48 px the icon does not read as one tree, or a round or squircle mask cuts the crown.
14g. It shows more than the tree, sky and a low grass line or a few bushes.

**All of 0.8**
14a. The balance and feel checks A to D of the 0.7 list fail again after the 0.8 fixes.
15. A first-time moment teaches more than one thing (onboarding-check.md).
16. Anything adds pressure or a reminder, or uses the network.

### A wider root field (0.8.1)

Simon (18:29 UTC): "bei den Wurzeln würde ich schauen, dass die Punkte viel weiter verteilt
sind, also dass man auch viel weiter fahren muss, um wirklich dann zu Patches von irgendwelchen
Ressourcen zu kommen. Ich will, dass man auch mal eine Wurzel weiter noch fahren muss in die
Richtung. Im Moment ist das sehr klein, das Volumen."

**What happened (0.8 numbers):** the soil reaches 14 m around the trunk, the 8 rich patches lie
from 2.2 m out and 3.2 m apart, and one calm tank reaches the whole topsoil (11 to 16 patches
with a fresh dot in reach). **What it means:** there is no journey; every patch is one short
drive away, so the night has no sense of distance and old roots never matter as starting points.

**The change (numbers [PLACEHOLDER] in tuning.md, the build thread tunes them):**
- The soil grows to about 30 m around the trunk (`Underground.EXTENT` 14 to 30; depth stays).
- Rich patches lie much further apart: the nearest ring from about 5 m, at least about 7 m
  between patches (`patch_near` 2.2 to 5, `patch_gap` 3.2 to 7), about 14 patches in all so each
  kind still has several, spread over near (5 to 12 m), middle (12 to 20 m) and far (20 to 30 m)
  rings. A calm night from the best start reaches about 2 to 4 of them; the far ring needs a
  root continued from an old root's tip over two or three nights, which is the "eine Wurzel
  weiter fahren" Simon asked for.
- Scattered dots thin out with the bigger volume (about 1200 in the whole field, so about a
  third of today's density), still within the 4000 loaded-dot budget.
- The price of distance halves (`distance_cost` 0.06 to 0.03), or a 25 m drive would cost about
  2.5x a metre by the trunk and far patches would never pay.
- Meadow signs: patches under the clearing keep their signs; patches beyond it show their sign
  at the clearing's edge in their direction (a clump of nettles by the forest edge), so a far
  patch can still be read from the day.
- The starter patch and the young tree's first week stay as they are, so the first days are
  not harder.
- Must hold (tuning.md "Days to finish" and items 1, 4, 7, 10a): steered months still 26 to 34
  (linden), never-steered still inside 10a, a night still 20 to 44 s from the tank, ending
  early still clearly loses.
- **Side roots (0.8.2) follow the new field:** their reach (2.5 m and 0.8 m) stays short; in the
  sparser field they will find fewer dots, which helps item 4. Where no dot is in reach, the
  second level also sprouts a few short tips (as the third does), so the fan still shows. No
  other change needed; the 0.8.2 check confirms.

### A shorter journal with doodles (0.8.2)

Simon (2026-09-30, 17:57 UTC): "Im Journal sollen weniger Punkte notiert werden. Und sie sollen
kürzer sein. Jede Seite soll ein Doodle haben zum Thema. Der Font in allen Punkten soll mehr nach
Handschrift aussehen. Dafür gerne einen guten kostenlosen Font runterladen."

- **Fewer, shorter entries.** A day page holds at most three entries, each one line of about
  eight words. What stays, in this order: the day's wish, a need the tree shows (care), and one
  find or visitor or milestone. Routine lines (numbers, "the tree grew", repeats of yesterday) go.
  Explanation pages (care, meadow hints, first sunset, tools) shrink to what the player needs to
  act, about a third of today's length.
- **A doodle per page.** Every page carries one small ink drawing about its topic (the wish's
  plant, the visitor, the tool, the leaf of a need), in the same ink as the existing sketches;
  the 0.7 wish drawings and `InkSketch` are the model.
- **A more handwritten font.** Body text moves from Patrick Hand to a freer, clearly handwritten
  free font under the SIL Open Font License, bundled with its OFL text in `ui/fonts`. Default:
  **Kalam** (Indian Type Foundry, OFL, readable at phone size); the build thread may pick another
  OFL hand if it reads better on the phone. Caveat stays for headings. "Clearer print" keeps
  Patrick Hand as the calm, legible choice.

## 0.8.2.2: Simon's 0.8.2.1 phone notes (2026-10-02)

Simon tested 0.8.2.1 on the phone (11:11 UTC, PC thread). Bugs go straight to the build thread
(the hitch and wrong camera picture when going back to the day, the shed's table objects

### Faster fast-forward

Holding still by day runs the clock at **8x** (was 4x; Simon: "doppelt so schnell"). Nothing
else in specs/fast-forward.md changes: game-time based, boosts fire at their hours, no result
changes. 8x means twice the sim steps per frame, so the frame rate while held is part of the
check.

### Dive by swiping up

The swipe direction between tree and roots is reversed (Simon): **swipe up** at sunset to dive
into the ground, **swipe down** to come back up after the night. Default taken here: the way
back reverses too, so the two gestures stay opposite. The journal's first-time line and any
hint arrow show the new direction.

### Roots go around old roots

A new root can no longer run into an existing root (Simon): when the tip would hit one, it
steers around it by itself and then carries on in the **same direction** the player was
holding.

- **What counts as an obstacle:** the main roots of earlier nights and the earlier part of
  tonight's root. Fine roots and side roots are thin and do not block (default taken here).
  The root a night starts from is ignored for the first metre.
- **The detour:** when the held heading would bring the tip within about 0.35 m of an old main
  root in the next metre, the tip bends to the side that needs the smaller turn (left, right,
  over or under; in the topsoil sideways first), keeps that distance while passing, and then
  eases back onto the held heading. The stick's direction is never changed, and the player does
  not need to steer.
- **Cost and pace:** detour metres cost as any other metre; the night's length rule is
  unchanged. Dots beside an old root stay reachable (the pickup reach of 0.7 m is larger than
  the gap).
- **Boxed in** (no way around within about 2 m, e.g. among many roots by the trunk): the tip
  slows and stops as at a rock, and the existing boxed-in rule applies.

### Gentler auto-steering, stopping at once, small roots everywhere

Simon, 11:14 UTC: the auto-steering toward dots is good but too strong; it makes sharp curves
and loses the original direction. Side roots should pick up the dots nearby when they can grow
far enough, and the player should be able to stop at once, after which small roots grow
everywhere from the life force left and collect the nearest nutrients.

- **Weaker pull:** the magnetism toward a fresh deposit ahead (`magnet_rate`, `magnet_radius`)
  turns the tip about half as fast (1.3 to about 0.6 rad/s) and only within about 1.2 m (was
  1.8 m). One deposit bends the heading by at most about 30 degrees; once the tip has passed or
  drunk it, the heading eases back to the direction the stick holds (with the stick let go, to
  the heading before the pull).
- **Stop at once:** "end root here" shows from the moment the night opens, also before a start
  point is picked or the root has moved. Ending before any root is grown spends the whole tank
  on small roots (below); there is no main root that night.
- **Small roots everywhere:** the leftover (or the whole tank, when ending at once) grows the
  second- and third-level side roots of specs/side-roots.md, but from the whole root network,
  not only tonight's root: the nearest fresh dots to any root are reached first, within the side
  roots' short reach (1.0 m + 0.05 m per point, at most 2.5 m), until the leftover's nodes run
  out. A side root that reaches a dot drinks it that night at the fine share (0.1). The node cap
  of about 250 a night still holds.
- **Balance:** ending early must still lose to steering (items 4 and 10a): the short reach and
  the fine share keep it behind. A small sim run (linden, seed 14, end_early against dots) checks

# Specs: the three 0.7 candidates

Written 2026-09-29. All three are new mechanics, so they belong to 0.7 and were asked first
(project rule). **Decided by Simon on 2026-09-29: the recommended option for all three** (marked
below). 0.7 is built only after the 0.6.x phone test.

## 1. A visible goal underground each night

**Purpose.** Give the night a direction the day already prepared. Today the daily wish ("today,
reach the damp patch in the west") only lives on a morning scrap, and nothing underground points
to it, so reading the meadow rarely turns into a plan.

**What the player should feel.** Curiosity and a small, quiet "found it". Not a task.

**Input.** None new: the day's wish (already seeded from the meadow's rich patches) and the
usual steering.

**Output.** Underground, the wish's patch glows a little warmer than the rest, faint at the edge
of the fog so it reads as "over there". Reaching it gives what the patch holds (it is a real,
slightly larger deposit placed by the same seeded generator, not a reward from nowhere) and a
small ink drawing in the diary.

**What working looks like.** On most nights a well-played root reaches it within the 40 to 60 s
night; aiming for it beats random steering (broken list 5).

**Soft failure.** Missing it costs nothing; the patch stays and still glows faintly the next
night, then fades when a new wish comes.

**Edge cases.** The patch behind rock or out of reach tonight: the wish picks only patches a
full calm tank can reach from some root start. A night without life force: the glow is visible
on the quiet visit, as a promise. Colour-blind players: the glow is warmth and a slow pulse, not
only hue.

**Tuning levers.** Glow distance, deposit size bonus (suggest 1.5x a normal rich patch), how
often a wish points underground.

**Risk.** This must never turn into a quest list (a pillar). No counter, no tick marks, no
"3 of 5".

**Question for Simon (answered).** Show the day's wish as a soft glow underground?
- **Glow and a real bigger deposit** (recommended; **chosen**): the meadow, the wish and the night become
  one plan, and a well-steered night pays.
- Glow only: a direction without any extra reward.
- Leave it as the morning scrap: the night stays as open as today.

## 2. Branches the tree marks for pruning

**Purpose.** Give the shears a reason every few days. Shade dieback already kills inner branches
slowly and silently (5 % of shaded tips a day); the player never sees it coming.

**What the player should feel.** A gardener's eye: "that branch is dying, I'll take it off."

**Input.** None new: cut mode as today.

**Output.** A branch that will die back soon shows it naturally a day or two ahead: its leaves
thin and turn dull, its bark greys. Cutting it before it dies returns the share described in
specs/care-and-pruning.md and wakes buds where there is light; left alone it dies back as today.

**What working looks like.** At most two or three marked branches at a time, visible from the
normal camera, and gone within a day or two either way.

**Soft failure.** Ignoring them is fine: the branch dies back as it does now.

**Edge cases.** Beech has no shade dieback (its quirk): no marks. Autumn: dull and grey are
harder to read among autumn colours, so the sign is sparse leaves first. The bonsai is not
included.

**Tuning levers.** Days of warning (1 to 2), the most marks at a time (2 to 3), how dull.

**Question for Simon (answered).** Should the tree show branches that are about to die, so you can cut them?
- **Natural signs only** (recommended; **chosen**): thin, dull leaves and grey bark; you learn to spot them.
- Natural signs plus a soft glow in cut mode: easier to find, a little more UI.
- No marks: shade dieback stays silent as today.

## 3. Bonsai tools on the windowsill

**Purpose.** The pillar says every menu is a real object. In the bonsai close-up the tools are a
paper menu today, while the shed around it is made of real objects.

**What the player should feel.** Handling real tools at a real windowsill.

**Input.** Tap a tool on the sill (watering can, pellet tin, shears, wire coil, a small trowel
for repotting), then tap the tree or the soil. Tap the tool again, or its place, to put it down.

**Output.** The same actions as today's paper menu; the chosen tool lifts a little and follows
the finger or cursor. First use of each tool still opens its journal page.

**What working looks like.** Every bonsai action reachable without the paper menu, one tap to
pick a tool; readable on the phone at arm's length.

**Soft failure.** Nothing new; bonsai care never harms (section 16).

**Edge cases.** Small phone screen: tools must be at least finger size in the close-up. The
repot moment asks only every seventh day: the trowel lies there always but only works then. The
"clearer print" switch labels the tools.

**Tuning levers.** Tool size and spacing on the sill, whether a first-time label shows.

**Question for Simon (answered).** Put the bonsai tools on the windowsill as real objects?
- **Real tools on the sill** (recommended; **chosen**): fits the shed, where every menu is an object.
- Keep the paper menu: less work, the close-up stays as it is.
- Both for a while: the tools plus their paper labels until each has been used once.
